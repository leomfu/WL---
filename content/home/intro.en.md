I'm Weiliang, based in Shanghai. Computer networking major, graduated in 2026 — not a programmer by training.

Most of my day is spent working alongside AI, not chatting with it. I use it to **read**: I dump in more source material than I could ever get through myself, have it follow rules I wrote to summarize, link concepts together, and periodically check for contradictions — so what I've learned doesn't just evaporate. I use it to **build**: an idea in plain language becomes running code, which I read line by line before it ever ships. And I use it to **learn English**: reading real articles instead of textbook English, having it unpack context and implied meaning where I get stuck, instead of just running it through a translator.

The stack that actually matters: **Claude Code** is the workhorse — writing code, running the rules behind my knowledge base. **Obsidian** holds what I know, **Notion** holds what I need to remember. **ChatGPT** is for daily questions and cross-checking — asking the same thing twice and seeing where the answers disagree usually points straight at what I still don't understand. **VS Code, GitHub, and Vercel** handle writing it, storing it, shipping it.

Knowing how to use AI isn't the hard part. Knowing whether what it gives you is actually right is. So I keep the process here, unedited: things I've understood, explained plainly; things I haven't, said plainly too; tools I only recommend after paying for them myself.
